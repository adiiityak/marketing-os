# Copy Editing

Use this skill to edit, review, or improve existing marketing copy — polishing, tightening, refreshing, or auditing content that already exists (as opposed to writing new copy from scratch, which is the `copywriting` skill).

## Seven Sweeps Framework

Run copy through these seven sweeps, in order. Each sweep has a specific focus, what to check, common issues, and a process to follow.

1. **Clarity** — Focus: can a reader understand this in one pass? What to check: jargon, ambiguous pronouns, buried subjects, sentence length. Common issues: passive voice, nested clauses, undefined acronyms. Process: read each sentence aloud; if you stumble, rewrite. After this sweep, every sentence should be understandable on first read.

2. **Voice and Tone** — Focus: does this sound like the brand and fit the context? What to check: consistency with brand voice guide, appropriate formality for the channel, personality bleed-through. Common issues: generic corporate tone, inconsistent voice across sections, mismatched register (too casual for enterprise, too stiff for social). Process: read against brand voice reference; flag lines that could belong to any company. After this sweep, the copy should sound distinctly like the brand.

3. **So What** — Focus: does every claim connect to a reader benefit? What to check: features stated without benefits, benefits stated without proof. Common issues: feature-dumping, assuming the reader will infer the "why it matters." Process: after every claim, ask "so what?" and make sure the answer is on the page. After this sweep, every feature should be paired with a benefit.

4. **Prove It** — Focus: are claims substantiated? What to check: superlatives ("best," "leading," "#1"), vague quantifiers ("many," "most"), unsupported claims. Common issues: marketing puffery with no backing, missing data/logos/testimonials/case study links. Process: flag every unsupported claim; add proof or soften the claim. After this sweep, claims should be backed by evidence or appropriately qualified.

5. **Specificity** — Focus: is the copy concrete or generic? What to check: vague nouns and verbs, generic adjectives ("great," "powerful," "innovative"), round numbers that could be exact. Process: replace vague words with specific, sensory, or numeric detail. After this sweep, generic language should be replaced with specifics.

6. **Heightened Emotion** — Focus: does the copy create feeling, not just convey information? What to check: flat description without stakes, missing urgency or aspiration, no connection to reader's pain or desire. Process: find the emotional core of each section and sharpen the language around it. After this sweep, key sections should evoke a clear emotional response.

7. **Zero Risk** — Focus: does the copy remove reasons to hesitate? What to check: missing guarantees, unclear pricing, unaddressed objections, no easy next step. Process: identify friction points and add risk-reversal language (guarantees, free trials, no-commitment framing). After this sweep, obvious objections should be pre-empted.

## Expert Panel Scoring

**How it works:** Assemble a panel of 3-5 relevant expert personas (e.g., a direct-response copywriter, a UX writer, a skeptical prospect, a brand guardian, a conversion specialist) and have each score the copy independently against the scoring rubric, then synthesize feedback.

**Recommended expert panels:**
- Landing page: conversion copywriter, brand voice guardian, skeptical prospect, UX writer
- Email sequence: direct-response copywriter, deliverability/spam-awareness reviewer, brand voice guardian
- Sales page: direct-response copywriter, objection-handling specialist, skeptical prospect, legal/claims reviewer

**Scoring rubric:** Score each dimension 1-5 — Clarity, Voice Fit, Persuasiveness, Proof/Credibility, Emotional Resonance, Call-to-Action Strength. Note specific lines driving low scores.

**When to use:** For high-stakes copy (homepage, pricing page, key email sequences) where multiple perspectives catch more than a single editing pass.

## Quick-Pass Editing Checks

**Word-level:** Replace weak/filler words with stronger alternatives — e.g., "utilize" → "use," "in order to" → "to," "very unique" → "unique," "leverage" → "use," "solution" → the actual noun, "just," "simply," "really," "very" (often deletable).

**Sentence-level:** Cut sentences that don't earn their place; break up sentences over ~25 words; convert passive to active voice; eliminate throat-clearing openers ("It's important to note that...").

**Paragraph-level:** One idea per paragraph; lead with the point, not the setup; check that transitions between paragraphs feel natural, not abrupt.

## Common Copy Problems & Fixes

1. **Feature-dumping** — Fix: pair every feature with a "so what" benefit.
2. **Generic corporate voice** — Fix: run the Voice and Tone sweep; inject brand-specific language.
3. **Weak or buried CTA** — Fix: make the CTA specific, benefit-oriented, and visually/structurally prominent.
4. **Unsubstantiated superlatives** — Fix: replace with specific proof or soften to defensible claims.
5. **Too long / not skimmable** — Fix: break into shorter paragraphs, add subheads, bullet only where genuinely list-like (avoid over-formatting).
6. **No emotional hook** — Fix: run the Heightened Emotion sweep on opening and closing sections.
7. **Ignores objections** — Fix: run the Zero Risk sweep; add FAQ or objection-handling section.
8. **Outdated references** (old pricing, deprecated features, old testimonials) — Fix: content refresh pass — verify every factual claim, date, price, and proper noun is current before publishing.

## Content Refresh Editing

When refreshing older content: verify all facts, statistics, and dates are current; check that product names, pricing, and features match current reality; update outdated screenshots or references; re-run the Seven Sweeps on any section being substantially rewritten; preserve SEO equity (don't change URLs or core headings unnecessarily).

## Working with Copy Sweeps

Apply sweeps in order for a full edit, but for quick edits, pick the 1-2 sweeps most relevant to the reported problem (e.g., "this feels flat" → Heightened Emotion; "nobody's converting" → So What + Prove It + Zero Risk).
