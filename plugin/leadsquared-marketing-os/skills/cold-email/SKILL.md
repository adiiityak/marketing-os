# Cold Email

Use this skill to write B2B cold outreach emails and follow-up sequences designed to get replies.

## Before Writing

Gather: who is the recipient (role, company, likely priorities), what is the product/offer, what specific trigger or context justifies this outreach now, and what is the single ask.

## Writing Principles

1. **Write like a peer, not a vendor.** Tone should read as one professional reaching out to another, not a sales pitch. Avoid corporate/marketing-speak entirely.

2. **Every sentence must earn its place.** Cold emails are read in seconds. Cut any sentence that doesn't move the reader toward the ask.

3. **Personalization must connect to the problem**, not just prove you did research. Naming their company or a recent LinkedIn post is not personalization unless it connects directly to why you're reaching out and the problem you solve.

4. **Lead with their world, not yours.** Open with something about the prospect's situation, industry, or challenge — not "I'm reaching out because we..."

5. **One ask, low friction.** End with a single, easy-to-answer ask (e.g., "worth a 15-min chat?" or "want me to send over details?") — not multiple asks or a hard commitment.

## Voice & Tone

Target voice: confident but not pushy, conversational, specific. Calibrate formality to the audience (more casual for startups/SMB, more polished for enterprise). What NOT to sound like: a mail-merge template, overly enthusiastic ("I'm SO excited to..."), or apologetic ("Sorry to bother you...").

## Structure

Common shapes for the email body:
- **Observation → Problem → Proof → Ask**: note something specific about their situation, name the problem it implies, offer brief proof you can solve it, ask.
- **Question → Value → Ask**: open with a sharp, relevant question, state the value you offer, ask.
- **Trigger → Insight → Ask**: reference a specific trigger event (funding, hire, launch), share a relevant insight, ask.
- **Story → Bridge → Ask**: brief relevant anecdote or customer story, bridge to their situation, ask.

## Subject Lines

Keep short (3-6 words), lowercase or sentence case (avoid title case, which reads as spammy), specific rather than clever. Avoid spam trigger words ("free," "guarantee," excessive punctuation). Reference the recipient's world, not your product name.

## Follow-Up Sequences

Plan 3-5 touches over 2-3 weeks. Each follow-up should add new value or a new angle, not just "bumping this up." Final follow-up can use a "break-up" approach (giving the prospect a graceful way to say not-now). Space touches roughly 3-5 business days apart; vary channel if possible (email + LinkedIn).

## Quality Check

Before sending, check: is the first line about them, not you? Is there one clear, low-friction ask? Would this survive being read on a phone in 5 seconds? Does it sound like it was written by a human to this specific person?

## What to Avoid

Generic templated openers ("Hope this finds you well"), leading with your company's features, multiple asks or vague asks ("let me know if you're interested"), overlong emails (aim under 150 words), attachments in the first touch, buzzword-heavy language.

## Data & Benchmarks

Reply rates vary widely by list quality and personalization depth; well-targeted, well-personalized cold email typically sees notably higher reply rates than generic blasts. Shorter emails and specific, low-friction CTAs generally outperform longer emails and open-ended asks.
