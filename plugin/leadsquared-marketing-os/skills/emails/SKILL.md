# Emails (Lifecycle / Drip Sequences)

Use this skill to design or optimize multi-email automated flows: welcome sequences, lead nurture, re-engagement/win-back, and onboarding email programs.

## Initial Assessment

Determine: sequence type (welcome, lead nurture, re-engagement, onboarding, campaign), audience context (where are they in the funnel/lifecycle, what do they already know), and goals (activation, conversion, retention, re-engagement).

## Core Principles

1. **One Email, One Job.** Each email should drive toward a single primary action; don't overload with multiple CTAs or goals.
2. **Value Before Ask.** Lead with something useful (insight, tip, resource) before asking for anything.
3. **Relevance Over Volume.** Fewer, more targeted emails outperform more frequent generic ones.
4. **Clear Path Forward.** Every email should make the next step obvious.

## Email Sequence Strategy

**Sequence length:** varies by type (see below). **Timing/delays:** front-load early touches (days 0-3) when engagement is highest, then space out. **Subject line strategy:** vary patterns across the sequence (curiosity, direct benefit, question, urgency) to avoid fatigue. **Preview text:** should complement, not repeat, the subject line.

## Sequence Types Overview

- **Welcome** — 5-7 emails over 12-14 days. Key emails: (1) immediate welcome + quick win, (2) core value/how-to, (3) social proof, (4) feature highlight, (5) check-in/feedback ask, (6-7) deeper engagement or upgrade nudge.
- **Lead Nurture** — 6-8 emails over 2-3 weeks. Key emails: (1) deliver lead magnet/promised value, (2) related educational content, (3) case study/proof, (4) address common objection, (5) soft product introduction, (6) stronger CTA, (7-8) urgency/last-chance framing.
- **Re-Engagement** — 3-4 emails over 2 weeks. Key emails: (1) "we miss you" + what's new, (2) value reminder/best content, (3) incentive or direct ask, (4) final "should we say goodbye" email.
- **Onboarding** — 5-7 emails over 14 days. Key emails: (1) getting started guide, (2) key feature walkthrough, (3) quick win celebration, (4) advanced feature intro, (5) check progress/offer help, (6-7) upgrade or expansion nudge.

## Email Types by Category

Onboarding emails (setup steps, feature intros), Retention emails (usage tips, milestones), Billing emails (renewal reminders, payment failures), Usage emails (usage summaries, limit warnings), Win-Back emails (re-engagement, special offers), Campaign emails (launches, promotions, newsletters).

## Email Copy Guidelines

**Structure:** clear single focus, scannable format. **Formatting:** short paragraphs, one primary CTA button, minimal design clutter. **Tone:** match brand voice; lifecycle emails can be more conversational than campaign blasts. **Length:** keep concise — most lifecycle emails should be readable in under 30 seconds. **CTA guidelines:** one primary CTA per email, action-oriented button copy, visually distinct.

## Output Format

When producing a sequence, structure as: (1) Sequence Overview — type, length, cadence, goal; (2) Per-Email breakdown — subject line options, preview text, send timing, body copy, primary CTA; (3) Metrics Plan — what to track per email (open rate, click rate, conversion) and what "good" looks like for this sequence type.

## Task-Specific Questions

Ask if unclear: what triggers entry into this sequence, what's the single most important next action, what data/personalization is available, what tool will send these (affects formatting/merge tags).

## Tool Integrations

Sequences may need to be built in tools like Customer.io, Mailchimp, Klaviyo, Resend, SendGrid, or similar ESPs — ask which platform if the format/merge-tag syntax matters.
