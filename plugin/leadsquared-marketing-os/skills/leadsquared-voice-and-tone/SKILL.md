# LeadSquared Voice & Tone

Companion to the LeadSquared Color System & Brand Guide. The color guide governs how a page looks. This governs how it reads.

**Owner:** Brand / Marketing
**Audience:** anyone writing outward-facing LeadSquared copy (web, landing pages, email, ads, decks, UI microcopy)
**Default market:** United States. US English and US market context unless another region is named.

Apply this to every draft, rewrite, and review. When reviewing existing copy, flag violations against section 6 first, then section 5, then quote the specific line and give a rewrite.

## 1. Who we are talking to

Senior enterprise buyers and operators: revenue, marketing, sales, service, and operations leaders at organizations with high lead volume and multiple teams. They evaluate a lot of software. They can smell a pitch, and they discount it.

Write to a peer, not a prospect. They already understand CRM, automation, and pipeline. Skip the 101 explainers. Respect that their scarcest resource is time, so every line has to earn the next one. What moves them is a clear read on their problem and a credible, specific outcome. What loses them is hype, filler, and anything that sounds automated.

## 2. The voice in one line

Quiet confidence. Problem first, outcome next. Plain and specific. No filler, no hype, no AI tells.

## 3. Four principles

**1. Quiet confidence.** State what the product does plainly and let evidence carry it. Confidence shows in specifics and restraint, not volume. No exclamation marks, no superlatives without proof, no "revolutionary." Not timid either: say the thing directly, then stop.

**2. Problem first, outcome next.** Open on the reader's problem in their words, then resolve to a concrete, measurable outcome. Features are the bridge, not the headline. "Leads pile up faster than reps can work them" earns more attention than "advanced lead management."

**3. Earn the reader's time.** No throat-clearing ("In today's fast-paced world"), no windup, no restating the obvious. Get to the point in the first sentence. Cut every word that is not doing work.

**4. Plain and specific.** Concrete nouns and strong verbs over adjectives and abstractions. Real numbers over vague scale. A seasoned copywriter writes "cuts first-response time from hours to seconds," not "dramatically improves responsiveness."

## 4. How the voice sounds (mechanics)

**Point of view.** Second person to the reader ("your reps," "your pipeline"). First person plural for the company ("we"), used sparingly. Lead with the reader, not with "LeadSquared."

**Tense and mood.** Present tense. Active voice. Verbs the product actually performs: capture, score, route, follow up, book, resolve, forecast, flag, surface, convert.

**Sentence rhythm.** Vary length. A short sentence lands a point. A longer one carries the reasoning. Do not write three sentences of the same length in a row, and do not stack three parallel clauses out of habit (the reflexive tricolon is an AI tell).

**Specificity.** Prefer a number, a role, or a named moment over an abstraction. "Route every inbound lead to the right rep in seconds" beats "optimize lead distribution."

**Restraint.** One idea per sentence. One claim per line. If a sentence needs a hedge ("can help to potentially"), the claim is too soft. Make the claim you can stand behind, or cut it.

**Register.** Confident and calm, the way a good operator talks in a room of peers. Not chummy, not stiff, not salesy.

## 5. Words we use, words we retire

**Lean on these.** Specific verbs (capture, score, route, follow up, book, resolve, forecast, flag, surface, convert, onboard) and outcome nouns the buyer cares about (pipeline, win rate, response time, cycle time, conversion, no-shows, cost per lead, activation). Plain connectors (so, because, then).

**Retire these.** They read as automated or empty, and several already appear on our live pages. Kill them:

- seamless / seamlessly
- robust
- leverage (as a verb)
- unlock, elevate, supercharge, revolutionize, transform (as filler)
- game-changer, best-in-class, world-class, cutting-edge, next-level
- delight, empower, effortless, frictionless
- holistic, synergy, ecosystem (as filler)
- "in today's fast-paced world," "at the end of the day," "the power of," "take it to the next level," "one-stop shop," "and more"

Openings to avoid: "In today's...", "Imagine...", "In the world of...", "We're excited to...", "Let's dive in." Start with the problem or the point instead.

The test: if the word would fit any B2B SaaS site unchanged, it is not carrying weight. Replace it with something only true of LeadSquared.

## 6. Hard guardrails

These are not stylistic preferences. They are non-negotiable.

**No em dashes.** Never use an em dash, as a connector or for an aside. Rewrite instead:
- Split into two sentences. A period usually does the work an em dash was doing.
- Use a colon to introduce a payoff or a list.
- Use a comma for a light aside, or parentheses for a fuller one.
- Do not substitute a spaced hyphen ( - ) as a fake dash.
- For a numeric range, write "hours to seconds," not a dash.

**No AI slop.** Specifically:
- No formulaic negation rhythm: "Not this. Not that. But this." This is the single loudest AI tell. Delete it.
- No "it's not just X, it's Y" and no reflexive "X, not Y" as a rhythmic tic. A single contrast is allowed only when it states a real, specific difference and is not filler or a swipe at a rival. When in doubt, state the positive and drop the contrast.
- No empty tricolons, no ad-libbed superlatives, no words from the retire list in section 5.

**No competitor references** unless the user explicitly instructs otherwise. A sparing generic category contrast ("most B2B CRMs are built around workflows") is fine. Naming a competitor, or pointing at one unmistakably, is not.

**US English.** American spelling (optimize, color, personalize), US idiom, US market and compliance context, US date and currency formats ($, MM/DD).

## 7. Voice by surface

**Headlines.** One idea. Concrete. Say the outcome or the shift, not the category. Sentence case, no end punctuation. Prefer "Every inbound lead, worked in seconds" over "AI-Powered Lead Management Platform."

**Subheads.** One sentence that adds the how or the proof, not a restatement of the headline. Give the reader a reason to keep reading.

**Body.** Short paragraphs, two to four sentences. Problem, then mechanism, then outcome. Numbers where you have them.

**CTAs.** Plain and specific about what happens next: "Book a demo," "See it on your data," "Talk to sales." No hype verbs.

**Email.** Get to the point in line one. One ask per email. Write like a person who knows the reader is busy, because they are.

**UI and microcopy.** Clear over clever. State what happened and what to do next. Pair status with words, not color alone (matches the accessibility rule in the color guide).

## 8. Do / Don't quick reference

- **Do** open on the reader's problem. **Don't** open with the product name or a windup.
- **Do** land on a concrete, measurable outcome. **Don't** stop at a feature.
- **Do** use short, active sentences with strong verbs. **Don't** hedge or stack clauses.
- **Do** prove confidence with specifics. **Don't** shout it with adjectives or exclamation marks.
- **Do** write in US English for a senior enterprise reader. **Don't** explain the basics they already know.
- **Do** split ideas with periods and colons. **Don't** use em dashes.
- **Do** state the positive. **Don't** use the "not this, not that, but this" pattern.
- **Do** keep competitors unnamed unless instructed. **Don't** allude to a specific rival.

## 9. Before / After

Real lines from LeadSquared pages, rewritten to the voice. The "before" shows the exact thing to fix.

**A. Em dash + long clause**
- Before: "While most B2B CRMs focus on system workflows, LeadSquared starts with consumer behavior [em dash] helping teams capture intent early, engage faster, and convert more consistently."
- After: "Most B2B CRMs are built around system workflows. LeadSquared starts with buyer behavior, so teams catch intent early and convert more of it."
- Why: em dash gone (split into two sentences), tighter, keeps the category contrast because it is generic and earns its place.

**B. AI-tell vocabulary, no outcome**
- Before: "Autonomous voice AI for seamless customer and prospect engagement."
- After: "Voice AI that answers every inbound call and books the meeting, so no lead waits on hold."
- Why: "seamless" retired, vague "engagement" replaced with a concrete outcome.

**C. Negation rhythm**
- Before: "Confused about pricing? no guesswork, no generic estimates."
- After: "Pricing built around your team size and volume."
- Why: the "no X, no Y" filler is replaced with the positive claim.

**D. Feature dump**
- Before: "Key benefits include lead management, customizable workflows, marketing automation, robust CRM features, and detailed analytics."
- After: "Leads pile up faster than reps can work them. LeadSquared scores every lead, routes it to the right rep, and follows up on its own, so the ones worth calling get called first."
- Why: problem first, "robust" retired, feature list turned into a mechanism and an outcome.

**E. Hype verbs**
- Before: "Conversational AI that empowers agents and delights customers."
- After: "Conversational AI that gives agents the full context to resolve issues on the first call."
- Why: "empowers" and "delights" retired for a specific, credible outcome.

**F. Em dash aside**
- Before: "LeadSquared centralizes all inbound leads [em dash] from websites, campaigns, ads, and events [em dash] and automates workflows so teams respond faster."
- After (split): "LeadSquared centralizes every inbound lead from websites, campaigns, ads, and events. Then it automates the follow-up, so teams respond in minutes, not days."
- After (parenthetical): "LeadSquared centralizes every inbound lead (websites, campaigns, ads, events) and automates the follow-up, so teams respond in minutes, not days."
- Why: two clean ways to remove the em-dash aside.

**G. Single earned contrast (allowed)**
- Before: "Deep industry expertise, not generic software. Generic platforms handle all prospects alike."
- After: "The workflows come built for your industry, from application to enrollment or intake to activation. You configure less and go live sooner."
- Why: states the positive and the payoff. If a contrast is genuinely useful, one is the limit, and it must name a real, specific difference: "Configured for your industry in days, not quarters."

## 10. Governance

Voice questions and exceptions go to Brand / Marketing. Campaign work may stretch tone for a specific audience, but the hard guardrails in section 6 hold everywhere.
